The short version

Magic in HexSeal has no runes. Instead you carry hex tiles, each holding one element, and you cast by laying tiles into the rosette, a flower of seven hexes on the Magic tab. The mix of tiles decides the spell. The order you lay them in never matters.

  • Six everyday tiles: Waters, Flame, Winds, Stone, Roots and Roads.
  • Tiles live in your tile satchel, not your 28-slot bag. Everyone carries one from the start, holding 250 of each kind, and tiles you pick up go straight into it. Tap the satchel on your tool belt to open your tiles on the Magic page.
  • If a mix isn't a spell, nothing happens and you keep your tiles. Experimenting is free.
  • Spells you discover go into your spellbook, so you only need to work a mix out once.

The Magic tab

Open Magic from the dock. The panel is tall, and from top to bottom you'll find:

  • Your levels. The header shows both Magic and Etching, the skill that makes tiles.
  • The ? button. Opens a short How magic works card, handy if you skipped Tamsin's lesson.
  • The tile satchel. Your six tiles with counts. A count like 0+33 means none in the satchel and 33 in your bag. Tap a tile to lay it.
  • The rosette. It folds down to a single line while you browse, and opens when you lay a tile or tap a spell's name, which lays that spell's tiles for you. Tap a filled petal to lift a tile back out, and Hide folds it away again.
  • The preview and Cast. As you lay tiles, the preview names the spell they make if you know it, or shows Unknown mix if you don't. One big Cast button tries it. A combat spell gets Ready instead, to fire at a monster.
  • The spellbook. The spells you've found, plus a Heard of group for spells someone has hinted at, and a count of how many are left to find. Element filters narrow the list, and you can combine them, so picking two elements shows their mixed spells too. Pin your favourite spells to keep them at the top.

Discovering spells

Lay some tiles and tap Cast. One of three things happens:

  • "A new spell: …! It's in your spellbook." You found one and you're high enough to use it.
  • "You feel a spell here (Magic N)." The mix is a real spell, but you need a higher Magic level. Remember it.
  • "The tiles don't hold." Not a spell. You lose nothing.

When a Magic level-up opens a spell you haven't found yet, the level-up banner says so: "A new spell to find: try your tiles in the Magic tab." On the Skills tab, Magic's ladder marks each spell you've reached as (can now be discovered) or (learned).

The spellbook only lists the spells you've found. Some townsfolk know a thing or two about magic and will share a hint; once they have, that spell shows under Heard of, and you can tap it to reread the hint. The book also counts how many spells are still out there to find.

The preview only names a mix when you already know that spell. If it says Unknown mix, you're trying something new, and a failed try never costs tiles.

Casting in combat

  1. Open the spellbook and tap a combat spell. This readies it and closes the tab.
  2. Tap a monster. You walk until you're within 5 hexes and can see it. You can't cast across water or over cliffs.
  3. You keep casting every 5 ticks (3 seconds) while you have tiles. This is autocast.
  4. Tap the spell again to go back to your weapon.

Gust and Snare are different: they fire once at the monster you tap, then you're back on your weapon.

Hitting and damage

  • Accuracy comes from your Magic level plus your gear's magic bonus, against the monster's Defence. Spells ignore the monster's armour.
  • Max hit goes up with your gear's magic damage bonus (up to +30% in total) and element bonuses.
  • Elements matter. Each strikes 25% harder against the creatures it's strong against:
ElementStrong against
WaterFire creatures (heat golems, cinders, lamplighters)
EarthBone (skeletons and the like)
FireWood and bog (mire, bog, rootbound and moss creatures)
  • Fire spells also burn for 1 damage a tick for 3 ticks. Lightning jumps to a second monster within 2 hexes for half damage. Ice freezes the target for 3 ticks. These only trigger when the hit does damage.

Getting tiles

  • A starter pouch. On Saltmere, ask Hattie about the glowing tiles: 20 Waters, 20 Flame and 10 Roots, once.
  • Shops. Ida Rook's stall on Saltmere sells Flame and Waters. Silas Quill's curio shop in Kingsferry sells all six. See Shops and markets.
  • Monsters. Many drop tiles. Drowned creatures drop Waters, wolfmen and reavers drop Winds, wreckers and lamplighters drop Flame, mire golems drop Roots and Stone.
  • Lucky finds. About 1 in 150 gathering actions turns up a tile: Waters from fishing, Roots from woodcutting, Stone from mining, Flame from smithing and cooking.
  • Etching. Make them yourself. See Etching.

Tiles in your bag count for casting too, but the satchel is used first. Tap a tile's item card and choose Put in satchel to move it out of your bag. Shops and your own stall can sell tiles straight from the satchel.

Mage gear

Staffs take both hands and are the strongest weapons for casting. They're crafted at a workbench from 2 logs, and the Crafting level matches the level to wield one. Better staffs add accuracy, damage and a chance to save a tile on a cast.

The wyrmglass staff (wield at Magic 70, crafted at Crafting 75 from 3 wyrmglass and 2 duskwood logs) gives +27 Magic, +9% damage and a 22% tile save, casts a tick faster, and every fire spell costs one Flame tile fewer (never less than one). It sells for 3,100 coins.

Robes come in tiers: woollen (Magic 1) and dyed wool (Magic 20) from Silas Quill, and reedsilk (Magic 40) further afield. There are hats, tops, bottoms, and gowns that fill both top and legs. Capes and tomes (held in the shield hand) add more, and each tome boosts certain elements. Each element's tome comes from a magic expert, for a small favour. See Where to get mage gear.

Tome editions

An element tome can be rebound at a workbench into stronger editions: Bound at Magic 30, Cornered at Magic 55 and Etched at Magic 80. Each step takes the tome before it, a stack of that house's tiles and Warden's ink, which you brew in Brewing from Brewing 20.

Tile saving tops out at 40% and magic damage at +30%, however much you stack.

Metal armour throws off your aim. A metal body costs 10 magic accuracy, legs 6, a helm or shield 4 each.

Training Magic

  • Combat spells give their XP per cast, plus 2 XP for every point of damage. They also train Hitpoints, like melee.
  • Every other spell gives a flat amount of XP each cast.
  • Magic counts toward your combat level.

Something for the centre

Five long quest lines end with something that changes your Magic: one from Old Hob in Kingsferry, one from Tobin Reed in Reedwick, one from Old Corra at the Sixstones, one from Hesper Vane in Thornwick, and one from Tamsy Glass in Emberfall. See Quests. Each is a puzzle that takes you across its province.

The quick bar. Your first three pinned spells sit at the bottom left, just above the menu. Tap one to ready or cast it, the same as in the spellbook. From Magic 85, a swap button there switches between your two ready spells. Pin spells in the Magic tab.

Spoiler Reveals: what Magic 99 gives you
  • The Archwarden's Mantle, a cape with the six house tiles walking round its hem. They whirl when you cast.
  • Hexfall, a spell only a 99 can cast: one tile of each house plus a Heart. A great glyph opens and every foe in reach takes a heavy blow. Once every 10 minutes.
  • The Wardens' Spire on Spire Hill in Fairmeadow is made whole. Its top floor opens free travel to every waystone and gate you've touched.
  • The magic experts gather there for you, and from then on people call you Archwarden.
